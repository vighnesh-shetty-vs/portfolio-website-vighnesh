Notes from the work

Short write-ups of things the projects taught me. Two minutes each.

Data quality · bank analytics The test that found two quirks in a 25-year-old bank dataset

I wrote a dbt test that says each day's balance must equal the previous day's balance plus that day's net flow. Simple rule, and it failed on about half the rows.

Two reasons. First, the dataset doesn't record the order of transactions within a day, so a naive running total breaks. Second, interest postings are rounded differently from balances: 763,838 account-days reconcile exactly, and 46,430 (5.7%) are off by at most 0.20 CZK.

What I changed: the test now accepts any intra-day order and a 0.50 CZK tolerance. It still catches real breaks. The lesson: a reconciliation test tells you how the data was produced, not only whether it is right.

Credit risk · leakage “Balance ever negative” predicts default perfectly. That's the problem.

The Berka dataset's own guide warns that, if you ignore time, “balance ever negative” separates good and bad loans perfectly. Of course it does: a client who stops paying goes negative after the loan.

So every loan feature in my warehouse is built only from transactions before the grant date, and a dbt test fails the build if any feature touches a later one.

The honest result: even with that rule, the 27 loans to already-overdrawn clients all defaulted. A real signal survives the time constraint; a leaky one doesn't.

Experimentation · fintech A winning A/B test I would only partly ship

The new neobank onboarding flow lifted KYC completion from 59.4% to 62.5% (+3.1 pp, 95% CI +1.9 to +4.4). On the primary metric alone, that's a ship.

But I had pre-registered a fraud guardrail, and it rose from 0.45% to 0.73% of signups (p = 0.004). And the gain wasn't even: +4.9 pp on Android, +1.0 pp on iOS (not significant), where the old flow was already working better.

The recommendation: roll out on Android only, where the benefit is real, with an extra fraud check. Guardrails and segments are what turn a test result into a decision a risk team will sign.

ML · imbalanced data SMOTE didn't help my churn model, and that's normal

On 7,043 telecom customers I compared plain logistic regression, class weighting and SMOTE, plus a simple contract-type rule as a baseline.

AUC was 0.83 with or without SMOTE. Oversampling changes where the threshold lands, not how well the model ranks customers.

Takeaway: class weighting gives the same ranking with less machinery. What mattered to the business was that the riskiest 30% of customers held 65% of churners.
